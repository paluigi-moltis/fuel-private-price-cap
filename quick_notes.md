# The voluntary fuel price cap: what the data shows

*Quick notes on the figures in `output/figures` — non-technical summary for policy audiences. Prices are daily averages for motorway-free ("stradale") stations; the voluntary cap took effect on 28 September 2026.*

- **The cap works where it is applied, and compliance spread fast.** On the first day only about one station in five priced at or below the cap (21% for both petrol and diesel, Figure 3). Within a week over half of all stations did, and by 7 October roughly 55% of petrol and 66% of diesel stations complied (Figure 3). Overall, more than two thirds of stations priced at or below the cap on at least one day in the first nine days. Before the cap, only 1–2% of stations were already at those levels — the shift is a genuine response, not a labelling effect.

- **Pump prices fell for consumers.** Average petrol prices dropped from about 2.11 EUR/l before the cap to 2.04 EUR/l by 7 October; diesel went from about 2.24 to 2.26 EUR/l, but this includes a deliberate 6 c/l increase on 6 October when the temporary excise discount expired (Figures 1–2). Adjusting for that tax change, underlying diesel prices moved down, not up.

- **Leader brands anchored the cap; the rest of the market followed.** Agip Eni and Q8 stations price almost exactly at the cap (Figure 8–9). Independent stations and smaller brands started with bigger cuts but then settled slightly above the cap leader level — by 7 October the gap between the cheapest and most expensive market segments had narrowed to roughly 2–7 cents per litre (Figures 1–2, 8–9). The cap has effectively become the market reference price.

- **Adoption is broad but geographically uneven.** Every province saw some compliance, but shares range from roughly one third of stations in the lowest-uptake provinces to over 70% in the highest (Figures 4–5). Uptake is driven by local competition, not just brand presence: stations with a compliant competitor nearby, or close to a cap adopter, are significantly more likely to adopt themselves. Where Independents dominate the local market, adoption is comparable to the leader brands' — competition, not company size, is the active force.

- **The 6 October excise step was absorbed without breaking the mechanism.** When the cap threshold for diesel rose with the tax change, most compliant diesel stations re-priced to sit just under the new, higher threshold within a day (66% vs 55% the day before, Figure 3). The cap adjusted as designed; there is no evidence of technical non-compliance or reporting artefacts.
